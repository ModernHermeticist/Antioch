# Next 100: writing + mixing

Each item names what changes and, for writing, the critic rule that would prove it (so the
best-of-six and the tests can hold it). Mixing items name the plugin that does it.

## A. Hook identity (1-14)
1. **Hook motif DNA**: extract the hook's 2-beat cell (rhythm + interval shape) and score its recurrence across the whole song (critic: `hook.identity`).
2. **Intro tease**: the hook's first cell, filtered/sparse, in the last 8 bars of the intro.
3. **Breakdown statement**: the full hook, slowed or on a different instrument, before the build (already partly there: make it reliable across kits).
4. **Build fragments**: the cell repeated and climbing in the build's last 8 bars (fragmentation, scored).
5. **Climax placement rule**: the 8-bar hook's highest note in bars 5-7, reached by step or a prepared leap, once.
6. **Second-drop payoff**: drop 2 lifts the climax (octave or a new peak note) instead of copying drop 1 (critic: `journey.payoff`).
7. **Call/answer contrast**: bars 1-2 and 3-4 share rhythm, differ in their ending; scored as "same start, different end".
8. **Hook rhythm signature**: one syncopation that's the hook's own (an anticipated beat 3, say), kept in every variation.
9. **Range per mood**: euphoric hooks span wider (an octave or more), dark ones narrower; critic bands per mood.
10. **Singability check**: no more than 2 leaps of a 6th or more per 8 bars; a held note at least every 2 bars.
11. **Hook vs vocal**: with a vocal, the hook steps back to the answers (it plays in the vocal's gaps only).
12. **Outro echo**: the cell, sparse and down an octave, in the outro (DJ-friendly identity).
13. **Hook bank scoring**: your saved hooks scored by the critic; the best offered first for New song.
14. **Counter-melody contour**: the counter moves against the hook's direction (contrary motion share scored).

## B. Groove and rhythm (15-28)
15. **Kick interlock score**: bass and arp onsets that land on the kick counted (bass wants the offbeats; critic `groove.kick`).
16. **Syncopation budget per style**: tech/psy allow more off-grid hits; uplifting keeps the pulse clear.
17. **Rhythmic layering**: no two melodic parts with the same rhythm in the same register (beyond `parts.crowd`).
18. **16th density curve**: drops busiest, breakdowns sparse, builds accelerating; per-part density scored by section.
19. **Ghost notes with intent**: ghosts only before strong hits (pickups), never on the kick.
20. **Push consistency**: if the bass pushes into chord changes, chords and arp push the same 16th (or none do).
21. **Swing lock**: one shuffle amount for the song, applied to bass, arp and percussion together.
22. **Fill vocabulary**: phrase-end fills chosen per style (rolls, stutters, rests) with variety across the song.
23. **Rest as groove**: a deliberate gap (an 8th) before the drop's first hit in the lead and arp.
24. **Accent map**: velocity accents shared by bass and arp on the same steps (the groove's shape).
25. **Gate follows the groove**: Kybalion's gate pattern matched to the song's kit rhythm by default.
26. **Half-time breakdown option**: the breakdown's rhythm parts move to half time for contrast.
27. **Build snare roll curve**: the roll's density follows an exponential, ending a beat before the drop.
28. **Groove score in the critic** (`groove.*` rules: interlock, density curve, push consistency).

## C. Harmony depth (29-42)
29. **Progression library per mood**: 15-20 progressions per mood (i-VI-III-VII, i-iv-VI-V, vi-IV-I-V...), the critic scoring "motion".
30. **Borrowed chords**: bVI, bVII and iv in major songs, IV in minor, placed in breakdowns and build ends.
31. **Pedal points**: a held bass note under changing chords in breakdowns and builds (tension), released at the drop.
32. **Tension curve**: a dissonance/instability score per bar, rising through the build, resolving on the drop's downbeat (critic `harmony.tension`).
33. **Cadence planning**: each 8-bar phrase ends on V or VII (open) or i (closed), chosen by where the song goes next.
34. **Breakdown reharmonisation set**: 3-4 reharms per mood (relative major, chromatic mediant) instead of one.
35. **Key-change preparation**: the final-drop lift always prepared by its new V (already there: score it).
36. **Suspensions that resolve**: sus4 resolving to 3 on chord changes, counted and required to resolve.
37. **Voice-leading budget**: top voice moves by step or common tone 80% of the time (critic `harmony.leading` tightened).
38. **Bass inversions**: first-inversion chords for a smoother bass line where the bass would leap more than a 5th.
39. **Chord rhythm variety**: some sections change chords twice a bar, others every 2 bars.
40. **Modal colour per mood**: Dorian for hopeful-minor, Phrygian touches for dark, Lydian lift for dreamy.
41. **Harmony against the vocal**: chords avoid a semitone against the vocal's held notes (vocal bus).
42. **Harmonic surprise budget**: one unexpected chord per song (deceptive cadence into the final drop), scored as a positive.

## D. Arrangement and journey (43-54)
43. **Whole-song critic**: every section judged, not just the first drop and breakdown.
44. **Energy curve target per structure**: intro 2, groove 4, breakdown 3, build 5→8, drop 9, outro 3 (scored against).
45. **Entrances staggered**: parts enter on 8-bar boundaries, at most two at a time (critic `journey.entries`).
46. **Exits before the drop**: the bar before the drop thins to its essentials (a vacuum).
47. **DJ intro/outro**: 16 or 32 bars of drums, bass and one element, mix-friendly (scored).
48. **Breakdown length vs drop length**: ratios that suit the style (scored).
49. **Second breakdown different**: a shorter, different-instrument second breakdown.
50. **Drop 2 arrangement change**: one new layer and one variation (counter, octave lead, harmony).
51. **Section-length variety**: not every section 16 bars (an 8-bar teaser drop, a 32-bar breakdown).
52. **Transitions matched to the change**: a riser into a big energy jump, a reverse into a breakdown (Transmutation hears the critic's energy curve).
53. **Silence moments**: a beat of silence used once per song (before the final drop).
54. **Arrangement A/B**: two arrangements (Ouroboros) compared by the critic as well as two writings.

## E. Layers and roles (55-62)
55. **Layer jobs**: each layer gets one job per section (pad, rhythm, counter, colour) and no two share a job.
56. **Orchestral rules**: strings, brass and choir voiced as a section (no doubled thirds, divisi spacing).
57. **Bells and guitar as answers**: they play in the hook's rests, not over it.
58. **Organ drone tied to the pedal point** (item 31).
59. **Sub vs bass split**: sub notes where the bass line is above E1, folded otherwise.
60. **Lead layers / octaves** only in drops 2+, a sixth or octave from the hook.
61. **Kick layers**: top and tail written to the kick pattern (top on every hit, tail on downbeats only).
62. **More families for the new roles**: 10 more each for bells, organ and guitar, filling mood × style gaps.

## F. The critic and your taste (63-72)
63. **Per-mood critic targets**: different ideal bands for euphoric, dark and dreamy.
64. **Critic explains in the UI**: the draft's weakest rules shown in Correspondence with "fix it" buttons.
65. **Fix it by rule**: a one-click repair for the top rules (re-fit hook ends, thin a crowded part).
66. **Taste weights per mood**: your picks learned separately for each mood.
67. **Reference songs**: score your own finished MIDI as references; the critic's targets move toward them.
68. **A/B of parts**: compare two hooks, or two basslines, in the same song (not just whole songs).
69. **Best-of-N per part**: the hook picked from N, then the bass for that hook, then the arp (greedy, faster).
70. **Critic speed**: render once, judge many (cache parts that don't change between candidates).
71. **Regression guard**: the tests hold every mood × style above its current score (no rule regresses silently).
72. **Critic report in the song pack** for collaborators.

## G. The mix follows the writing (73-90)
73. **Written roles to Quintessence**: each exported part tells its track's Quintessence its role (no guessing).
74. **Section energy to mix moves**: the critic's energy curve drives Quintessence's section EQ and Prima Materia's suggestions.
75. **Hook-aware masking**: Quintessence on pads dips at the hook's notes while the hook plays (already partly: make it note-exact from the MIDI).
76. **Counter-melody space**: the counter's track gets a presence dip when the hook plays.
77. **Bass/kick by pattern**: the kick-bass ducking follows the written bass pattern (where they collide).
78. **Arp width by register**: higher arps wider, lower narrower, from the written register.
79. **Breakdown space**: Antioch reverb grows with the breakdown's density drop (written, not detected).
80. **Build automation from the critic's curve**: filter and width rides shaped by the written build.
81. **Drop impact**: a 50 ms pre-drop gap plus transient lift on the drop's first hit (from the arrangement).
82. **Vocal pocket**: chords voiced away from the vocal's range (writing) plus Solve et Coagula's dip (mix).
83. **Layer loudness budgets**: layers sit under the core parts by role-specific amounts.
84. **Choir/strings reverb send by section** (bigger in breakdowns).
85. **Effects bus behaviour per section**: returns ducked harder in drops, opened in breakdowns.
86. **Sub-bass mono and phase** checked against the written sub line.
87. **Mud check from the notes**: Mentalism warns when the written parts stack below 200 Hz.
88. **Harshness from the notes**: high stacked parts (bells, candy, lead octave) flagged before mixing.
89. **Translation preview**: the song's MIDI rendered through a reference synth set for a quick listen.
90. **Mix critic**: Mentalism scores the mix with the same report style as the writing critic.

## H. The mix plugins' musical sense (91-100)
91. **Quintessence presets per new role** reviewed (bells, organ, guitar, effects bus) with targets.
92. **Effects bus in Mentalism's balance**: returns counted as space, not as instruments.
93. **Reverb tail tempo-sync** to the song's tempo in Antioch by section.
94. **Delay throws on hook phrase ends** (Antioch reads the hook's rests).
95. **Kybalion gate pattern library** matched to kits.
96. **Vibration drive by section** from the energy curve.
97. **Sidechain shapes by style** (pumping uplifting, subtle progressive).
98. **Label-ready check reads the writing**: Ouroboros' checks include the critic's journey.
99. **Premaster headroom by arrangement**: the loudest section's density predicts the peak.
100. **One "Make it better" button**: critic + mix checks, the top three fixes applied, undoable.
